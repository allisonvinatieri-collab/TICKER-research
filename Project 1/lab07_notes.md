# Lab 07 — Comparable Means Defensible

## Reopen and explain

My DCF range is driven mainly by FCFF growth, WACC, terminal growth, and the terminal value; cash, debt, and diluted shares then convert enterprise value to per-share value. One question I still need to investigate is whether a higher peer P/E reflects durable growth and returns, or simply temporary earnings differences.

## Define / Discover

P/E is price per share divided by diluted EPS: the price investors pay for one dollar of earnings. It helps compare differently sized companies because it scales the share price by earnings, and it complements a DCF by providing a market-based cross-check. A lower P/E is not automatically better: it may reflect weaker expected growth, greater risk, lower earnings quality, or a declining business. P/E is least useful with negative earnings, unusual or nonrecurring profits, or companies with materially different growth, risk, and capital structures.

## Represent

Policy before the prices: use AutoNation as a public auto-retail peer; use Group 1 as a qualified public auto-retail peer; exclude Asbury because it is the target. Group 1 needs qualification for its different brand mix, geography, financing and insurance income, acquisition pace, leverage, and earnings quality.

## Evolve and reflect

Removing Group 1 should lower Asbury's implied price because Group 1 has the higher P/E. With only AutoNation remaining, the result is a reference estimate rather than a range because there is no peer variation left to define a minimum-to-maximum band. The peer-implied band is not proof of fair value: the peers and Asbury can be mispriced, and their growth, margins, risk, and capital structures can differ.

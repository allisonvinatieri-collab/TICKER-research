# Lab 12 Partner Discussion — ANF

## D — The question

My current conclusion is **watch-defer** on Abercrombie & Fitch, or ANF. The company has a strong balance sheet and continued revenue growth, but I would wait before initiating a position because growth is unbalanced across its brands and operating margins have weakened. The key issue is that Hollister is growing strongly, while the Abercrombie brand is declining. My DCF shows potential upside under its assumptions, but the operating evidence makes those assumptions uncertain. I would research whether Abercrombie sales stabilize and whether tariff and mix pressure on margins improves before changing my conclusion.

## R — The full analysis route

### 1. Target selection

- I selected ANF because it is a consumer retail company with two distinct brands, clear brand-level sales disclosures, recent financial filings, and a relatively simple capital structure.
- My initial view was watch-defer. I liked that ANF had revenue growth and a net-cash balance sheet, but I wanted to determine whether that growth was broad-based and sustainable.
- The company is suitable for analysis because its main operating question is clear: can both brands grow while maintaining margins?

### 2. Company and evidence

- ANF earns revenue by selling apparel and accessories through its Abercrombie and Hollister brand families, using stores and digital channels.
- My primary source was ANF’s Fiscal 2025 Form 10-K, filed March 26, 2026. The reporting period ended January 31, 2026.
- Financial statement amounts are reported in U.S. dollars and generally in thousands; I converted figures to millions when presenting the model.
- The most important evidence is:
  - Fiscal 2025 sales increased 6% to $5.266 billion.
  - Comparable sales increased 3%.
  - Hollister sales increased 15% to $2.743 billion, with 13% comparable-sales growth.
  - Abercrombie sales declined 1% to $2.524 billion, with comparable sales down 7%.
  - GAAP operating margin declined from 15.0% to 13.3%.
  - Management cited about $90 million of adverse tariff impact, plus unfavorable product and channel mix.
  - ANF ended the year with about $759.5 million of cash and no interest-bearing borrowings.
- The company is financially sound, but the quality of growth is the concern. Hollister is carrying growth while Abercrombie is weakening.

### 3. Pro-forma

- My five-year pro-forma begins with Fiscal 2025 revenue of $5.266 billion.
- My base revenue-growth assumptions are 5%, 4%, 3%, 3%, and 3% over Years 1 through 5. I used a higher first-year growth rate than the 3% reported comparable-sales growth because total Fiscal 2025 sales grew 6%, and management’s store, digital, and partnership initiatives could add growth beyond comparable sales. However, I fade growth to 3% because I do not assume unusually strong growth lasts forever.
- My gross-margin assumptions are 61%, 61.5%, 62%, 62%, and 62%. The economic reason is that Fiscal 2025 gross margin was pressured by tariffs and mix, but my base case assumes partial recovery rather than immediate full recovery.
- The model links revenue to gross profit, gross profit to SG&A and EBIT, EBIT to taxes and net income, and net income plus non-cash charges and working-capital changes to cash flow. Capex is modeled as 4.5% of revenue.
- The base pro-forma produces Year 5 revenue of $6.284 billion, EBIT of $896.1 million, and unlevered free cash flow of $581.0 million.
- The accounting checks passed in every forecast year. The balance-sheet check was essentially zero, and cash remained above the $500 million minimum-cash assumption.

### 4. Valuation

- I used an FCFF DCF, meaning I value operating cash flows before debt financing, discount them at WACC, then convert enterprise value to equity value.
- My DCF assumptions were:
  - WACC: 9.0%
  - Terminal growth: 2.5%
  - Debt: $0
  - Non-operating cash: $638.0 million in the reverse-DCF record
  - Share basis: 44.864 million diluted weighted-average shares in that record
  - Valuation price date: September 9, 2026
  - Saved market price: $152.08 per share
- That DCF estimated a value of about $193.27 per diluted share, compared with the saved market price of $152.08.
- I also ran the five-year pro-forma model using a different share basis: 48.476 million diluted shares from Fiscal 2025. Its base output was $233.01 per share.
- I am not averaging the $193.27 and $233.01 estimates because they do not use the same inputs or share basis. The $193.27 DCF uses TTM FCFF and the later 44.864 million share count, while the pro-forma valuation uses the Fiscal 2025 share base and its own forecast structure. This is an unresolved consistency issue I would fix before treating either number as a final target price.
- My reverse-DCF result indicated that the September 9 market price implied a uniform reduction of about 6.08 percentage points in each of the model’s five FCFF-growth assumptions. In other words, the market price reflected a much weaker cash-flow path than my base DCF.
- A limitation is that the peer-valuation file in my workspace is for Asbury Automotive, not ANF. It is not an appropriate ANF peer set, so I am not using it as evidence for ANF’s valuation. I need to build an ANF-specific peer group before presenting a peer multiple valuation.

### 5. Sensitivity and drivers

- The sensitivity analysis holds all other assumptions fixed and changes one driver at a time.
- My base case uses revenue growth of 5%, 4%, 3%, 3%, and 3%, and gross margin of 61%, 61.5%, 62%, 62%, and 62%.
- For revenue growth, I tested a downside case that is two percentage points lower each year and an upside case that is two percentage points higher each year:
  - Base value per share: $233.01
  - Lower-growth value per share: $221.84
  - Higher-growth value per share: $244.80
  - Total value-per-share span: $22.96
- The causal chain is: lower revenue growth leads to lower revenue, then lower gross profit and EBIT, then lower free cash flow, and finally lower DCF value.
- For gross margin, I tested a downside case two percentage points below the base path and an upside case two percentage points above it:
  - Base value per share: $233.01
  - Lower-margin value per share: $224.47
  - Higher-margin value per share: $241.56
  - Total value-per-share span: $17.09
- The causal chain is: a lower gross margin reduces gross profit directly on every dollar of sales, which reduces EBIT, taxes, free cash flow, and value.
- In my tested ranges, revenue growth had the larger effect on value than gross margin: a $22.96 span versus $17.09.
- That does not prove revenue growth is always more important. The ranking depends on the ranges tested, the forecast horizon, and the rest of the model’s assumptions.
- The main message is that both drivers matter, but ANF’s ability to produce sustained, broad-based revenue growth is especially important in this model.

### 6. Interpretation

- My supported conclusion remains watch-defer.
- The DCF suggests potential value at the saved September 9 price, but I do not treat that as enough evidence to buy because the model assumes growth and partial margin recovery while current operating results show a declining Abercrombie brand and margin pressure.
- My view has become more cautious since selecting the company. Initially, the 6% sales growth and net-cash balance sheet were the most visible signals. After building the model, I think the more important question is whether that growth is durable across both brands.
- I would become more constructive if:
  - Abercrombie comparable sales stabilize or become positive.
  - Hollister continues positive comparable-sales growth.
  - Operating or gross margins stabilize as tariff and mix pressures improve.
  - An ANF-specific peer valuation and a reconciled DCF share basis support the same general conclusion.
- I would become more negative if Hollister slows sharply while Abercrombie remains negative, or if margin pressure persists despite sales growth.
- The next assumption I would research is my revenue-growth path. Specifically, I would look at the next quarterly results to see whether growth is still coming mainly from Hollister or whether Abercrombie is recovering.
